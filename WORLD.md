# Phone-dor — World Bible

A grim, grounded, phone-native party RPG in the spirit of *Betrayal at Krondor*,
set in the shared world of **DiggyDwarves** and **Rootwake**.

Status markers: **[canon]** = stated by the designer. **[repo]** = established in
DiggyDwarves/Rootwake docs. **[proposal]** = suggested here, not yet confirmed.
**[open]** = a question still to settle.

---

## 1. The shape of the world

- **[canon]** A small planet with earthlike conditions and earthlike gravity.
  It is meant to be *walkable*. Traveling the deep roots, Hulda can circle the
  globe in about half an hour. A party on foot, stopping to eat and fight, takes
  days to go from one karst to the next.
- **[canon]** Physics bends to the mythic. Light curves around the small world,
  so from a point roughly equidistant from three towers, the peaks of all three
  can be seen at once.
- **[canon]** Where the physical karst ends, the **metaphysical tower** begins.
  It stretches up into the atmosphere. A **stream of heavenly water** falls out
  of the sky onto each karst (the "ethereal stream", **[repo]** in DiggyDwarves
  and Rootwake). By day the sky shows where the distant towers stand. **There is
  always a karst on the horizon.**
- **[repo]** There are **eight karsts, "the Teeth"**, set at the corners of a
  cube on the globe. Twelve cube edges join each karst to its three neighbours.
  In DiggyDwarves these are the buried magitech subway lines, "the cage" around
  the Chaos core.
- **[canon]** The karsts are massive magitech constructions, like DiggyDwarves'
  karst rather than Rootwake's small limestone spire. They have power cores,
  manufacturing halls, miners, traders and an economy.

### The cube as a map **[repo positions, proposal reading]**

```
              Hush ─────────── Keystone
             ╱ │               ╱ │
     Wallspire ─────── Emberflask│
         │     │           │     │
         │   Moot ─────────│─ Anviltooth
         │   ╱             │   ╱
     Greencrown ─────── Dynamo
```

- **Greencrown** (Hulda's karst) is **antipodal to the Keystone** (Delvira's).
  The two sisters sleep at opposite ends of the world. **[proposal: use this]**
- **The Moot** neighbours **Greencrown, Anviltooth and the Hush**. It is two
  roads from the Keystone, by way of Anviltooth or the Hush.
- **Each road along a cube edge is one chapter** (see §6). There are 12 roads.

## 2. Cosmology and the cycle

- **[repo]** Chaos is a formless, timeless void that *cannot be defeated*. Each
  planet is a sealed piece of Chaos: a Chaos core under a crust of life, which is
  the contested frontier of Order. The universe is "a dead-dreaming nightmare
  trying to wake up — one karst, one planet."
- **[canon]** The world cycles through **rise and fall**. It waits for the day
  the eight heroes choose to **recede**: to give up their essence and knowledge
  so that humanity carries it on. DiggyDwarves calls this **the Diminishing**,
  in which each karst becomes a "university".
- **[repo]** Every cycle fails in its own way: betrayal, love of the world,
  madness, greed. Chaos builds up in a hero as **greed** ("dragon-sickness").

### The incarnations of the eight

1. **[canon] The dragons.** Long ago the eight were dragons. They ruled with
   wisdom and power, then fell to fighting over the riches of the world.
2. **[canon] The dwarves / the eight heroes.** In their latest incarnation they
   gathered and **delved deep**, where magic runs stronger and more chaotic, and
   **fell to bickering**.
3. **[canon] The hurling.** To keep this cycle from failing like the others,
   the Eighth, **Delvira**, gave herself wholly to chaos magic. In a display of
   immense power she **flung her seven siblings across the planet** to sleep in
   their karsts.
4. **[canon] The long sleep.** The seven sleep. Delvira rules (§4). Phone-dor
   is set early in this sleep (§5).
5. **[canon] Delvira's death.** She is old, and dies before the seven wake. She
   lies in a **glass coffin, like Snow White.**
6. **[canon] The awakening (Rootwake / DiggyDwarves).** The seven wake, and
   **Delvira wakes in her coffin when Hulda does.** As they remember and grow,
   so does she.

### Where the essences went **[canon]**

- When the dragons fell, they **slept on their hoards**, and their essences
  merged with the earth.
- When Delvira cast the seven out, **their essences entered the earth too.** At
  least some of them **found their way back into their old dragon bodies**,
  which now **stir and manipulate the people on the surface.**
- So the dragons now waking are partly the sleeping heroes themselves: their
  oldest selves, the incarnation that fell to greed. **[proposal]** This is the
  game's buried twist. The party fights the dragons' influence without knowing
  the dragons are, in part, the very heroes everyone prays will return.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Artificer | [open] | Dynamo | lightning, light | [proposal] lamp-keepers. The only city that still lights its streets at night, and the lamps keep going dark |
| Smith | [open] | Anviltooth | metal, the forge | [proposal] a guild-monastery of smiths. The best steel in the world, rationed out to whoever pays |
| Steward | [open] | **The Moot** | governance | [canon] **bureaucracy. The books are kept on every facet of everything** |
| Mason | [open] | Wallspire | building | [proposal] a builders' order. Their bridges and walls hold the roads together, and the masonry is starting to fail |

- **[canon]** The other seven cities are smaller and specialized, with **more of a
  monastery feel** than the Keystone's grandeur.
- **[repo]** Between incarnations a hero sleeps as a **dwarf-shaped stone statue**
  on top of their karst. Medieval people know these as relics, shrines and
  pilgrimage sites.
- **[open]** The six other heroes' names are recorded in the designer's other
  Claude projects, which this repo can't see. Paste them in to fill the table.

### Living memory **[canon]**

People speak of the eight **in hushed tones**. It has been just long enough that
only the **eldest great-grandparents** remember, as children, one of the eight
touching their lives. These memories are the game's main source of hero-lore:
told at hearths, in inns, by the dying.

- **The tree and the wolves.** Hulda stepped out of a tree and brought a
  village's failing crops back to life, then taught them **crop rotation**. A
  hero in **shining metal armour** (the Smith) joined her, and together they
  fought off a pack of **demonic dire wolves** threatening the village.
- **The airship and the speaking-box.** The Artificer arrived in a village by
  **airship**, with amazing automata. He left a **wind-up electromechanical
  device** that let you hear and speak to people in other villages. That is how
  the village survived an outbreak of **consumption**: a doctor from across the
  world told them, over the device, how to make medicines from herbs. **The
  doctor had been taught by the Alchemist himself.**
- **[proposal]** These relics still exist in the Phone-dor era, mostly broken or
  misunderstood: a speaking-box no one can wind, rotation rules kept as rote
  ritual, a wolf-pelt hung in a longhouse. The decline shows in what people have
  forgotten how to use. Recovering or repairing them could be side quests.

## 4. Delvira and the city of thieves

- **[canon]** Delvira has absorbed the knowledge of all seven siblings. She has
  ruled her corner of the world as an **unopposed god-queen**, and her city-state
  is far more advanced than the rest: a **steampunk Renaissance** riddled with the
  touch of chaos.
- **[canon]** The city is backstabbing politics, black sorcerers, swindlers and
  con artists: **a city of thieves around an ancient spire.**
- **[canon]** In this incarnation she is **very old, bitter and hateful**. She is
  mostly content to watch the court drama play out. She resents her siblings,
  hopes they'll wake, and dreads it.
- **[canon]** **She has cast herself as the villain, so that the seven will unite
  against her instead of fighting each other.** This settles DiggyDwarves'
  "mercy disguised as betrayal" thread in favour of mercy, though no one in the
  world knows it.
- **[canon]** The rich merchants and the noble descendants of the spire's
  inhabitants court her favour. The best they can hope for is that she lets them
  do as they wish. **If any displease her, she dips her finger into the stew pot
  and gives it a stir, pushing them under the bubbling brew.**
- **[proposal]** In the field, "Delvira's stir" is the Krondor-style plot engine.
  A merchant house falls overnight, a guild's ledgers burn, a rival's fortune
  turns. The party never sees her hand, only the ripples.
- **[open]** City name. Is "the Keystone" the spire, and the city something else?

## 5. The age of Phone-dor: a world in decline

- **[inferred from canon, confirm]** **When:** roughly three generations (about
  80–90 years) after the hurling, because the eldest still remember the eight
  walking among them as children. The seven have only just gone quiet, and
  Delvira's long reign is nearing its end. Rootwake comes centuries later.
- **[canon]** Phone-dor is set **before Rootwake**. The karst-cities still hold
  together. They are the **upper layers of what Hulda will later find as ruins.**
  By Rootwake's time the Keystone's city is barren, a Mordor or Isengard, and the
  Hush's last mystics eat lotuses and dream of infinity.
- **[canon] The problem is chaos rising, not towers waking.** It shows up as:
  - more danger on the roads;
  - people behaving erratically, more self-seeking and short-sighted;
  - the wisdom of the heroes being forgotten, so crops die and masonry collapses;
  - resources dwindling;
  - the powerful hoarding instead of caring for the workers who make their wealth.
- **[canon] The dragons are waking.** They sense that human civilizations have
  grown corrupt and must be brought low, reset to primeval levels.
- **[canon] A dragon cult** feels them coming and piles up hoards of stolen
  treasure, hoping to win favour when the dragons rise to level civilization.
- **[repo]** Other chaos creatures: **abominations** (chaos leaks that twist
  people, animals and plants), chaos demons, and deep-forest demonic entities
  (Rootwake).
- **[proposal]** Remnant peoples of the old karst maintenance crews live in the
  deep places: Greeblins (industrial, shoddy work), Snerks (archivists and
  peacekeepers; the DiggyDwarves rule stands: no greedy-trickster framing) and
  the Hollowed (chaos-crystal mystics). Humans rarely see them.

### Seeds planted for Rootwake **[proposal]**

Rootwake's docs leave "**whose inscriptions?**" open. Phone-dor's people carved
them. Anything this game builds (a waystone, a Moot ledger, a party's grave) can
show up as a ruin Hulda finds centuries later.

## 6. The game's frame

- **[canon]** The protagonists try to **hold everything together against all
  hope**. They send **parties of adventurers** out to quell the rising chaos and
  to **reconnect the karst-cities that have gone quiet or turned hostile.**
- **[canon]** **Each chapter is the journey from one karst-city to another.**
- **[proposal] Home base: the Moot.** The Steward's bureaucrats keep the books on
  everything, so they are the first to notice the numbers going wrong: grain
  tithes falling short, couriers not returning, a city's letters stopping. A
  ledger-keeper sends out the party. Each chapter opens with a Moot dispatch, like
  Krondor's chapter cards.
- **[proposal]** The campaign walks the cube. Chapter 1 might be Moot → Anviltooth,
  and the endgame is the road into the Keystone. Twelve roads, nine-ish chapters,
  and the party never takes every road.
- **[proposal]** The party's untrusted outsider (Krondor's Gorath role) is someone
  born in the Keystone's city: a thief, a defrocked sorcerer, or a fallen
  noble-house heir.

## 7. Core loop for the first slice: travel and camp

- **Roads between karst-cities.** These are node-to-node paths with points of
  interest: waystations, villages, ruins, shrines, river crossings. You choose a
  route. Each leg costs hours.
- **Clock and upkeep.** Time of day, rations, health and stamina (Krondor's
  combined pool), fatigue (Krondor's fatigue rules are documented in
  canassa/betrayal-at-krondor `docs/GAME_MECHANICS.md`).
- **Chaos taint.** It works like sickness, but slides toward greed and short-sighted
  choices. Emberflask's cures are the counter.
- **Camp.** How many hours to rest, who keeps watch, who cooks, whether to light a
  fire (warmth versus being seen).
- **Navigation by sky.** The heavenly falls and the towers' sky-marks work as the
  compass. Fog and storms hide them.
- **Tone.** Terse second-person text, as in Rootwake: *"Rain. It wears at you out
  here."*
